# Solar Energy

- Solar energy is radiant energy from the sun, a fully renewable energy resource.
- It is used to provide daylight, electricity, and heat.
- There are four primary ways solar energy is harnessed: indirect use for light and warmth, solar PV, direct solar thermal heat, and solar thermal electricity (concentrating solar power).

## Solar Photovoltaic (PV) Technology

- Solar PV technology converts solar energy to electrical energy.
- The US Solar Energy Technologies Office (SETO) focuses on photovoltaic research.
- The National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) provides solar resource data, tools, and maps.
- The American Solar Energy Society (ASES) is an industry organization.

## Solar Thermal Electricity (Concentrating Solar Power)

- Solar thermal electricity, also known as concentrating solar power, harnesses solar energy to generate electricity.
- The International Energy Agency (IEA) Solar Heating & Cooling Programme focuses on solar thermal heating and cooling.

## Solar Energy Economics and Costs

- Lazard provides Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) data for solar PV installations.
- Costs of US solar PV installations vary based on size.
- Costs of US solar PV have changed over time.

## Advantages of Solar Energy

- Utility-scale solar PV has a very low Levelized Cost of Energy (LCOE) relative to fossil fuels.
- Solar energy has a relatively short implementation timeframe from project start to electricity generation.
- Rooftop solar PV requires no transmission and uses no additional land.
- Solar thermal heat is a low-cost option for heating buildings and certain industrial processes.

## Disadvantages and Challenges of Solar Energy

- Grid integration challenges arise due to intermittency and the "duck curve" effect.
- Inconsistent or decreasing financial incentives can hinder adoption.
- "NIMBY" (Not In My Backyard) and "BANANA" (Build Absolutely Nothing Anywhere Near Anything) concerns can create opposition.
- US economic sanctions against panel manufacturers in China can impact supply chains.
- Lack of solar PV recycling capacity at end of life poses a challenge.
- Rooftop solar PV faces economic viability threats from net metering reforms and utility rate redesigns.
- Solar thermal heat may not reach high enough temperatures for certain industrial processes.

## Environmental Impact of Solar Energy

- Solar energy has a low environmental impact.
- Water usage is minimal.
- Land use and habitat impact can be significant.
