# Solar Energy & Energy Systems

**Current Energy Landscape**
- The content is part of a larger learning hub focused on energy, stemming from Stanford University’s Precourt Institute for Energy.
- The hub offers courses, lectures, and resources for understanding energy systems.
- Topics covered include fossil fuels, renewable energy sources, and grid integration.

**Solar Energy Overview**
- Solar energy is harnessed in four primary ways: photovoltaic (PV), solar thermal electricity (concentrating solar power), direct solar thermal heat, and passive solar design.
- Global solar PV electricity generation has seen significant growth in recent years.
- Solar energy is a key component of the transition to sustainable energy systems.

**Solar PV Technology**
- Solar PV converts sunlight directly into electricity using semiconductor materials.
- PV technology has seen continuous productivity gains, leading to lower costs.
- Declining energy storage costs are improving the integration of solar PV into the grid.
- Financial incentives, such as investment tax credits, support the deployment of solar PV.
- Rooftop solar PV offers benefits such as reduced transmission needs and land use.

**Solar Thermal Electricity (Concentrating Solar Power)**
- Concentrating solar power (CSP) uses mirrors to focus sunlight and generate heat, which drives turbines to produce electricity.
- CSP can provide dispatchable power, meaning it can be turned on and off as needed.

**Solar Thermal Heat**
- Direct use of solar thermal heat is a low-cost option for heating buildings and certain industrial processes.
- Solar thermal heat can be used for water heating, space heating, and industrial process heat.

**Environmental & Economic Considerations**
- Solar energy has a low environmental impact, with minimal water use and greenhouse gas emissions.
- Land use can be a concern, but it can be mitigated through careful planning and siting.
- The cost of solar PV has decreased significantly in recent years, making it competitive with fossil fuels.
- Intermittency (the variable nature of sunlight) is a challenge for solar energy, but it can be addressed through energy storage and grid integration.
- The US economic sanctions against panels manufactured in China are a concern.
- Lack of solar PV recycling capacity at end of life is a growing issue.
- Net metering reforms and utility rate redesign threaten the economic viability of rooftop solar PV.

**Challenges & Opportunities**
- Grid integration challenges due to intermittency and the “duck curve” (a pattern of electricity demand that resembles a duck) need to be addressed.
- NIMBY ("Not In My Backyard") and BANANA ("Build Absolutely Nothing Anywhere Near Anything") concerns can hinder the deployment of solar energy projects.
- The lack of solar PV recycling capacity at end of life is a growing concern.

**Data & Statistics (as of 2023/2024)**
- Global solar PV electricity generation has increased significantly in recent years.
- The US and China are leading countries in solar PV installed capacity and generation.
- Solar PV accounts for a growing share of global electricity generation.
- Costs of US solar PV installations have decreased significantly in recent years.
- Global solar thermal heat capacity has also increased, but at a slower rate than solar PV.
