### **History and Key Developments in Solar Energy**  
The evolution of solar energy technology has been marked by significant milestones, innovations, and applications across various industries. Below is a structured overview of key events and advancements in the history of solar energy:  

---

#### **1. Early Discoveries and Inventions**  
- **Photovoltaic Effect (1839):** French physicist **Edmond Becquerel** discovered that light could increase electricity generation in a solution with metal electrodes, laying the foundation for the **photovoltaic (PV) effect**.  
- **Selenium Solar Cells (1873–1883):**  
  - **Willoughby Smith** identified selenium’s photoconductive properties in 1873.  
  - **William Grylls Adams and Richard Evans Day** demonstrated selenium’s ability to generate electricity from sunlight in 1876.  
  - **Charles Fritts** created the first selenium-based solar cells in 1883, though they were inefficient.  
- **Silicon PV Cell (1954):** **Daryl Chapin, Calvin Fuller, and Gerald Pearson** at **Bell Labs** developed the first practical **silicon photovoltaic cell**, achieving **4% efficiency**. This marked the true beginning of modern solar technology.  

---

#### **2. Major Events in Solar Energy History**  
- **Solar Panels in Space (1950s–1960s):**  
  - **1958:** The **Vanguard I satellite** used a 1-watt solar panel to power its radio.  
  - **1964:** NASA launched the **Nimbus spacecraft**, the first satellite to run entirely on a **470-watt solar array**.  
  - **1966:** NASA deployed the **Orbiting Astronomical Observatory**, powered by a **1-kilowatt solar array**.  
- **First Solar-Powered Residence (1973):** The **University of Delaware** constructed **"Solar One"**, a hybrid solar thermal and PV building, and pioneered **Building-Integrated Photovoltaics (BIPV)**.  
- **Solar-Powered Airplanes:**  
  - **1981:** **Paul MacCready** built the **Solar Challenger**, the first solar-powered aircraft, which flew across the **English Channel**.  
  - **1998:** NASA’s **Pathfinder** reached **80,000 feet**.  
  - **2001:** NASA’s **Helios** achieved **96,000 feet**.  
  - **2016:** **Bertrand Piccard** completed the first **zero-emissions flight** with **Solar Impulse 2**, the world’s largest solar-powered airplane.  
- **Presidential Solar Installations:**  
  - **1979:** **Jimmy Carter** installed solar panels at the **White House**.  
  - **1981:** **Ronald Reagan** removed them.  
  - **2010:** **Barack Obama** reinstated solar panels and a solar water heater at the White House.  

---

#### **3. Technological Advancements and Efficiency Records**  
- **1957–1960:** **Hoffman Electronics** improved PV efficiency from **8% to 14%**.  
- **1985:** The **University of New South Wales (UNSW)** achieved **20% efficiency** for silicon cells.  
- **1999:** **National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL)** and **SpectroLab Inc.** created a solar cell with **33.3% efficiency**.  
- **2016:** **UNSW** broke the record with **34.5% efficiency**.  

---

#### **4. Cost Reductions Over Time**  
- **1956:** Solar panels cost **$300 per watt**.  
- **1975:** Cost dropped to **$100 per watt**.  
- **2016:** Prices fell to as low as **$0.50 per watt**.  
- **Annual Decline (1980–Present):** Prices dropped by **at least 10% annually**, making solar increasingly accessible and driving widespread adoption.  

---

#### **5. Modern Applications and Accessibility**  
- **Solar-Powered Technologies:** Today, solar energy powers everything from **satellites** to **airplanes**, **residential homes**, and **industrial applications**.  
- **EnergySage Marketplace:** Platforms like **EnergySage** enable consumers to compare bids from vetted installers, access **solar calculators**, and explore **heat pump technology** for heating and cooling.  
- **Global Impact:** Solar installations have grown exponentially, with over **1 million U.S. installations** by 2016, and continued expansion into new markets.  

---

### **Conclusion**  
The history of solar energy reflects a journey from scientific curiosity to a cornerstone of renewable energy. Key milestones—such as the invention of the silicon PV cell, space applications, efficiency breakthroughs, and cost reductions—have transformed solar from a niche technology into a mainstream, affordable, and sustainable energy source. As innovations continue, solar energy is poised to play an even greater role in global energy systems.
