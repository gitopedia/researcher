**I. History of Solar Energy**

While modern solar technology is relatively recent, harnessing the sun’s power dates back centuries:

* **Ancient Use (7th Century BC onwards):**  Early civilizations used sunlight to create fire with magnifying glasses. Greeks and Romans used mirrors (“burning mirrors”) to concentrate sunlight for religious ceremonies and potentially ignition. Chinese civilization also documented similar practices.
* **Early Architectural Integration:**  “Sunrooms” were incorporated into buildings to maximize sunlight exposure, notably in Roman bathhouses. The Anasazi people built cliff dwellings oriented to capture solar warmth.
* **17th-19th Century Research:** Scientists began experimenting with solar-powered ovens for long voyages and even solar-powered steamboats.
* **The Photovoltaic Effect (1839):** Edmond Becquerel discovered that light could generate electricity when shone on certain materials, laying the groundwork for photovoltaic (PV) technology.
* **Selenium-Based Cells (1873-1883):** Willoughby Smith discovered selenium's photoconductive properties, leading to the creation of the first selenium-based solar cells by William Grylls Adams, Richard Evans Day, and Charles Fritts.
* **Silicon Solar Cells (1954):** Daryl Chapin, Calvin Fuller, and Gerald Pearson at Bell Labs created the first silicon photovoltaic cell, marking a significant breakthrough. This cell had a 4% efficiency, far lower than modern cells but a crucial first step.
* **Space Exploration (1958 onwards):** Solar panels were first used to power satellites like Vanguard I, demonstrating their viability in space applications. NASA played a key role in developing solar arrays for spacecraft.
* **First Solar Residence (1973):** The University of Delaware constructed the first solar-powered building, integrating both solar thermal and PV technologies.
* **Efficiency Advancements (1957-2016):**  Significant improvements in solar cell efficiency occurred over the decades, with records broken repeatedly, reaching over 34.5% in laboratory settings by 2016.
* **Solar-Powered Aircraft (1981-2016):**  From Paul MacCready’s Solar Challenger to the Solar Impulse 2, solar-powered aircraft demonstrated the potential for sustainable aviation.
* **White House Solar Panels (1979-2010):** President Jimmy Carter installed solar panels on the White House, later removed by President Ronald Reagan, and reinstalled by President Barack Obama.

**II. Current Solar Technology & Market**

* **Cost Reduction:** Solar panel prices have dramatically decreased over the past few decades, making solar energy increasingly accessible. Prices have fallen from approximately $300/watt in 1956 to as little as $0.50/watt today.
* **Market Growth:**  The declining cost of solar has fueled significant market growth, with over one million solar installations in the U.S. as of early 2016.
* **EnergySage Platform:**  EnergySage is a platform that simplifies solar shopping, allowing consumers to compare bids from vetted installers and estimate potential energy savings.

**III. Related Technologies & Concepts**

* **Photovoltaic (PV) Technology:** The direct conversion of sunlight into electricity using semiconductor materials.
* **Solar Thermal:** Utilizing sunlight to heat water or air for heating, cooling, or electricity generation.
* **Building Integrated Photovoltaics (BIPV):** Integrating solar cells directly into building materials like roofs and facades.
* **Heat Pumps:**  Clean heating and cooling technology that transfers heat rather than generating it, offering high efficiency.
* **Net Metering:** A billing mechanism that credits solar energy system owners for the electricity they add to the grid.
