**Solar Heating and Cooling Systems: Overview and Key Components**

**1. Passive Systems**  
Passive solar systems rely on architectural design and materials to harness solar energy without mechanical components. Key types include:  
- **Direct Gain**: South-facing glass (double-glazed in the Northern Hemisphere) allows sunlight to enter, heating floors and walls (thermal mass) during the day, which release stored heat at night.  
- **Thermal Storage Wall**: A massive wall (with water/masonry) behind south-facing glazing absorbs and stores solar heat during the day, releasing it at night.  
- **Solar Greenhouse (Sunspace)**: Combines direct gain in a greenhouse with a thermal storage wall between the greenhouse and the main house, providing heat for both spaces.  
- **Roof Pond**: Water containers with movable insulation collect and store solar heat. Insulation is opened during winter days to absorb heat and closed at night to retain it; in summer, the process is reversed for cooling.  
- **Natural Convective Loop**: A collector below the living space heats air, which rises to provide warmth. Used in thermosiphon systems for hot water, where heated water rises into an upper storage tank.  

**2. Active Systems**  
Active systems use mechanical components (pumps, fans) to circulate heat. Key elements include:  
- **Collectors**:  
  - **Flat-Plate Collectors**: A black surface under glass absorbs solar radiation, trapping heat via the greenhouse effect (up to 160°F/71°C).  
  - **Evacuated Tube Collectors**: Tubes with a vacuum insulation layer achieve higher temperatures (up to 350°F/177°C).  
  - **Parabolic-Trough Collectors**: Mirrors focus sunlight onto a receiver tube, reaching up to 750°F (400°C) but require sun tracking.  
- **Storage**: Stores heat for use when sunlight is unavailable.  
- **Distribution**: Pumps/fans circulate heated air/water.  
- **Backup Energy Source**: Supplements when solar energy is insufficient.  

**3. Applications**  
- **Heating**: Hot water (25% of home energy use), space heating, industrial/agricultural processes.  
- **Cooling**: Solar heat drives refrigeration systems or dessicant cooling for year-round use.  

**4. Benefits and Adoption**  
- Over 1 million U.S. residences and 20,000 commercial buildings use passive solar design.  
- Active systems offer higher efficiency for specific applications but require more maintenance.  
- Solar energy is renewable, reducing reliance on fossil fuels and mitigating environmental impacts.  

**5. Temperature Ranges by Collector Type**  
- **Flat-Plate**: Up to 160°F (71°C).  
- **Evacuated Tube**: Up to 350°F (177°C).  
- **Parabolic-Trough**: Up to 750°F (400°C).  

This structured approach highlights the design, components, and applications of solar heating and cooling systems, emphasizing the balance between passive efficiency and active performance.
