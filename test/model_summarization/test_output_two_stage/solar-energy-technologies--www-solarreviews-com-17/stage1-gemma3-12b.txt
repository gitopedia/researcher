# Early Discoveries and Innovations (1839-1905)

- **1839:** Alexandre Edmond Becquerel, a French physicist, created the first solar cell, known as a photovoltaic cell, demonstrating the ability to carry an electric current from light. This discovery is credited with the discovery of the photovoltaic effect.
- **1883:** Charles Fritts, an American inventor, created the first functioning solar modules using selenium wafers. Willoughby Smith discovered that selenium was photovoltaic. These early panels had an energy conversion rate of only 1%.
- **1888:** Edward Weston received the first U.S. patent for a "solar cell," which spurred further research.
- **1901:** Nikola Tesla received a U.S. patent for “the method of utilizing, and apparatus for the utilization of radiant energy,” essentially the first U.S. patent for solar panels.
- **1905:** Albert Einstein published a paper on the "photoelectric effect," officially proving how the sun creates energy through solar cells, earning him the Nobel Prize in 1922.

# Development and Mass Production (1954-1970s)

- **1954:** Gerald Pearson, Daryl Chapin, and Calvin Fuller at Bell Labs created the first high-power silicon solar photovoltaic (PV) cell, significantly increasing energy conversion efficiency.
- **1963:** Sharp Corporation, a Japanese electronics company, began mass production of silicon solar cells, installing a 242-watt PV array on a lighthouse.
- **1964:** NASA launched the first Nimbus satellite with a 470-watt PV array.
- **1970s:** Research drove down PV costs by 80%, allowing for various solar panel applications to be tested.
- **1973:** The University of Delaware built "Solar One," one of the world’s first PV-powered buildings, combining PV panels and solar thermal energy.
- **1976:** Kyocera began production of thin-film silicon ribbon crystal solar modules, simplifying the manufacturing process.
- **1977:** The U.S. Department of Energy established the U.S. Solar Energy Research Institute (now the National Renewable Energy Laboratories - NREL) for renewable energy research.

# Government Initiatives and Technological Advancements (1999-2015)

- **1999:** Germany launched the "100,000 Solar Roofs" program, a $500 million project to support the installation of PV systems, pivotal in developing a residential solar industry. Japan had a similar program a few years prior.
- **2005:** The Energy Policy Act created a 30% investment tax credit (ITC) for commercial and residential solar energy systems.
- **2006:** The California Public Utilities Commission launched the California Solar Initiative (CSI), a $3 billion subsidy program for solar incentive programs. The CSI program closed in 2016.
- **2007:** Global investment in clean energy exceeded $100 billion, with solar energy as the leading clean energy technology.
- **2008-2012:** The global cost of solar modules fell from $5 per watt to $1 per watt.
- **2012-2015:** The cost of residential solar systems became affordable for the average American household.
- **2015:** Tesla announced the launch of the Powerwall, a lithium-ion storage product for homeowners, initially priced at $3,500. Tesla has released four variations of Powerwall models since 2015.

# Recent Developments and Current Status (2022-Present)

- **2022:** The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA) was passed, extending the federal solar tax credit until 2034 and providing funding for domestic solar manufacturing.
- The average cost of a residential solar system is between $16,500 and $21,000 before incentives.
- Some of the best places to go solar in the U.S. are Massachusetts, Colorado, Washington D.C., and Maryland.
- Financing options include solar loans, leases, and power-purchase agreements.
- California ranks #1 for the solar market in the U.S., with over 46,874 Megawatts (MW) of solar currently installed.
- Over 92% of residential installers are also offering energy storage installations.
