# Renewable Energy Overview and Technologies  

## **Solar Energy**  
- **Historical Use**: Ancient civilizations utilized sunlight for heating and lighting.  
- **Key Developments**:  
  - First solar collector (1767) by Horace-Benedict de Saussure.  
  - 1950s: Solar energy research expanded under the IPCC's focus on climate change mitigation.  
  - Modern advancements: Solar PV (photovoltaic) systems became mainstream in the 2000s.  
- **Current Statistics**:  
  - Solar PV generated **1,300 terawatt-hours (TWh)** in 2023.  
  - Growth of **26%** in solar PV capacity in 2022 (per International Energy Agency).  
- **Corporate Adoption**: Companies like GPT Group (via on-site solar arrays) and Celestica (geothermal + solar) integrate solar to meet ESG goals.  

## **Wind Energy**  
- **Historical Use**: Windmills used for grinding grain and pumping water since 200 BCE.  
- **Key Developments**:  
  - First wind turbine for electricity (1887) by Professor James Blyth in Scotland.  
  - 1904: Charles F. Brush built the first automatic wind turbine in the U.S.  
  - 1980s: Commercial wind farms emerged.  
- **Current Statistics**:  
  - Global wind energy capacity reached **2,100 TWh** in 2023.  
  - Offshore wind farms now account for 10% of global wind energy.  
- **Technological Innovations**:  
  - Floating wind turbines for deep-sea installations.  
  - Offshore wind farms (e.g., Hornsea Project in the UK).  

## **Hydropower**  
- **Historical Use**: Water wheels used for mechanical power since ancient Rome.  
- **Key Developments**:  
  - 1882: First hydroelectric plant at Niagara Falls.  
  - 1900s: Francis turbine (invented in 1848) became the standard for hydroelectric generation.  
  - Pumped storage hydropower (1890s): Stores energy by moving water between reservoirs.  
- **Current Statistics**:  
  - Hydropower accounts for **16% of global electricity** (2023).  
  - Largest dams: **Itaipu Dam** (Brazil/Paraguay, 14 GW) and **Three Gorges Dam** (China, 22.5 GW).  
- **Role in Grid Stability**:  
  - Provides 90% of global energy storage capacity.  
  - Balances intermittent renewable energy (e.g., solar/wind) via pumped storage.  

## **Geothermal Energy**  
- **Historical Use**: Hot springs used for bathing since the Paleolithic era.  
- **Key Developments**:  
  - 1830: First commercial geothermal baths in Hot Springs, Arkansas.  
  - 1904: First geothermal power plant (Lardarello, Italy) by Prince Piero Conti.  
  - 1970s: Enhanced Geothermal Systems (EGS) developed to access heat in dry rock.  
- **Current Statistics**:  
  - Geothermal provides **2.6% of global electricity** (2023).  
  - Leading countries: U.S., Indonesia, and the Philippines.  
- **Corporate Adoption**:  
  - Celestica (Romania) transitioned to geothermal to reduce natural gas reliance.  

## **Bioenergy**  
- **Historical Use**: Wood and plant oils used for heating and lighting since prehistoric times.  
- **Key Developments**:  
  - 1897: Rudolf Diesel demonstrated vegetable oil in his engine.  
  - 2000s: Biofuels (ethanol, biodiesel) gained traction as alternatives to fossil fuels.  
  - 2020s: Renewable diesel (produced via hydrotreating) emerged as a "drop-in" fuel.  
- **Current Statistics**:  
  - Global biofuel production reached **160 billion liters** in 2023.  
  - Renewable diesel reduces greenhouse gas emissions by **80%** compared to petroleum diesel.  
- **Innovations**:  
  - Fourth-generation biofuels using algae and food waste to address land-use conflicts.  

## **Corporate and ESG Integration**  
- **ESG Goals**: Companies like IBM, Celestica, and GPT Group use renewable energy (solar, geothermal, biofuels) to meet sustainability targets.  
- **Data Management**:  
  - IBM Envizi™ software streamlines ESG data tracking and reporting.  
  - Renewable energy projects are monitored via APIs and sustainability software.  

## **Future Trends**  
- **Energy Storage**: Pumped storage hydropower and EGS will expand renewable energy integration.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - Floating solar farms and offshore wind turbines for higher efficiency.  
  - AI-driven grid management to optimize renewable energy distribution.  
- **Policy Drivers**:  
  - Global regulatory frameworks (e.g., EU Green Deal, U.S. Inflation Reduction Act) incentivize renewable adoption.  

## **Key Challenges**  
- **Intermittency**: Solar/wind require storage solutions (e.g., batteries, pumped hydro).  
- **Land Use**: Bioenergy and solar farms compete with agriculture.  
- **Geographic Limitations**: Geothermal and hydropower depend on natural resources.
