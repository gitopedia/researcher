# Renewable Energy Sources

# Historical Overview
- Renewable energy sources have been utilized throughout human history, evolving from basic applications to sophisticated technologies.
- Early uses included harnessing sunlight for torches by the Greeks and Romans, and the invention of solar collectors for cooking in 1767.
- The late 1800s saw the development of the first commercial solar water heaters and solar cells capable of converting light into electricity.
- The formation of the International Solar Energy Society in the 1950s marked the beginning of the modern age of solar research.
- Windmills were used as early as 200 BC in China for pumping water and in the Middle East for grinding grain.
- Hydropower has been used for mechanical purposes since ancient times, with the Greeks using water wheels to grind wheat.
- Geothermal energy was utilized for bathing in hot springs since the Paleolithic period, with the first commercial use occurring in 1830 in Hot Springs, Arkansas.
- Bioenergy, primarily through wood burning, has been a staple energy source throughout human history, and plant oils were used for lighting before the 19th century.

# Solar Energy
- The world’s first known solar collector was invented in 1767.
- Solar thermal energy, concentrating sunlight to create heat, was widely used in Israel by the 1960s.
- The energy crises of the 1970s spurred investment in solar technology as an alternative to fossil fuels.
- Since the 1990s, tax incentives and government policies have fueled growth in the solar energy industry.
- Large-scale solar power plants and distributed solar electricity (rooftop panels) have become increasingly common.
- In 2022, solar photovoltaic generation reached nearly 1300 terawatt hours, a 26% increase from the previous year.
- Companies are increasingly installing on-site solar photovoltaic arrays to meet ESG goals.

# Wind Energy
- Simple windmills in China were used to pump water as early as 200 BC.
- Vertical axis windmills were used in the Middle East to grind grain by 200 BC.
- The Dutch adapted windmills for industrial uses like paper making and lake drainage.
- James Blyth built the world’s first wind turbine in Scotland in 1887.
- Charles Brush and Poul la Cour also developed turbines in Ohio and Denmark, respectively, before the end of the 19th century.
- Denmark emerged as an early leader in commercial wind power due to supportive government policies.
- Utility-scale wind farms were installed in California in the 1980s, followed by Germany and Spain in the 1990s.
- Today, wind turbines generate a significant amount of electricity globally, with over 500 terawatt hours generated in 2023.
- Offshore wind energy is also growing in importance.

# Hydropower
- Hydropower has been used for mechanical purposes since ancient times.
- Pumped storage hydropower, dating back to the 1890s, stores energy by pumping water uphill and releasing it to generate electricity when needed.
- This system helps fill gaps in energy supply during peak demand.

# Geothermal Energy
- Geothermal energy stems from heat within the Earth.
- The first commercial use of geothermal energy occurred in 1830 in Hot Springs, Arkansas, with public baths heated by hot springs.
- The world's first district heating system using geothermal energy was installed in Boise, Idaho in 1892.
- In 1904, an Italian prince, Piero Conti, powered light bulbs using geothermal steam from Tuscany.
- Technological advances are being explored to expand geothermal energy production to more locations.

# Bioenergy
- Bioenergy is derived from organic materials like plants and animals.
- Wood was the primary fuel for heating and cooking throughout much of history.
- Rudolf Diesel experimented with vegetable oils in his diesel engine in 1897.
- Ethanol has been used as an automotive fuel for over a century.
- Scientists are working on using waste materials and algae to improve biofuel production.
- Renewable diesel, a drop-in fuel compatible with existing diesel engines, is gaining popularity.

# Future Trends & Technology
- Data management is becoming increasingly important for companies transitioning to renewable energy.
- ESG reporting software helps companies track and manage their sustainability efforts.
- Technological advancements are expected to drive further growth in renewable energy production.
- Companies are investing in on-site renewable energy systems to meet ESG goals and reduce their carbon footprint.
- The integration of renewable energy sources with energy storage systems is crucial for ensuring a reliable and sustainable energy supply.
