### **Comprehensive Summary of Renewable Energy Sources and Their Historical Development**  

---

#### **1. Solar Energy**  
- **Historical Milestones**:  
  - **1767**: The first solar collector was invented, marking early attempts to harness solar energy.  
  - **Late 1800s**: The first commercial solar water heater was developed, laying the groundwork for modern solar thermal applications.  
  - **1900s**: The invention of the photovoltaic (PV) cell by Bell Labs in 1954 enabled the conversion of sunlight into electricity.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - The **1970s energy crisis** spurred research into solar energy, leading to increased adoption of solar panels.  
  - **1990s**: Tax incentives and policy support accelerated the growth of solar PV systems globally.  
- **Current Applications**:  
  - Solar PV capacity reached **1,300 terawatt-hours (TWh) in 2023**, according to the International Energy Agency.  
  - Companies like **GPT Group** use on-site solar PV systems to meet ESG (Environmental, Social, Governance) goals.  

---

#### **2. Wind Energy**  
- **Historical Milestones**:  
  - **Ancient times**: Windmills were used in Persia and China for irrigation and milling.  
  - **19th century**: The first modern wind turbines were developed in the U.S. and Europe.  
  - **1904**: The first geothermal power plant (not wind) was built in Italy, but wind energy research gained momentum in the 20th century.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - **1970s–1980s**: Denmark pioneered large-scale wind farms, while the U.S. and Germany invested in turbine technology.  
  - **2000s**: Offshore wind farms emerged, leveraging stronger and more consistent wind resources.  
- **Current Applications**:  
  - Wind energy accounts for **9% of global electricity generation** (2023 data).  
  - **Offshore wind farms** contributed **18% of global wind capacity growth in 2022**, per the International Energy Agency.  

---

#### **3. Hydropower**  
- **Historical Milestones**:  
  - **Ancient times**: Water wheels were used for milling and irrigation in Mesopotamia, China, and Rome.  
  - **1890s**: **Pumped storage hydropower** was developed to store excess energy, enabling grid stability.  
  - **Early 20th century**: The first large-scale hydropower plants were built in the U.S. and Europe.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - **20th century**: Advances in turbine efficiency and dam construction expanded hydropower capacity.  
- **Current Applications**:  
  - **Itaipu Dam** (Brazil/Paraguay) and **Three Gorges Dam** (China) are the world’s largest hydropower plants, generating **14,000 MW** and **22,500 MW**, respectively.  
  - Hydropower provides **16% of global electricity** (2023 data).  

---

#### **4. Geothermal Energy**  
- **Historical Milestones**:  
  - **Paleolithic era**: Hot springs were used for bathing.  
  - **1830**: The first commercial use of geothermal energy occurred in Hot Springs, Arkansas.  
  - **1904**: The first geothermal power plant was built in Tuscany, Italy, by Prince Piero Conti.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - **20th century**: Enhanced geothermal systems (EGS) were developed to access deeper heat sources.  
- **Current Applications**:  
  - Geothermal energy meets **1.5% of global electricity demand** (2023 data).  
  - Companies like **Celestica** use geothermal power to reduce reliance on fossil fuels.  

---

#### **5. Bioenergy**  
- **Historical Milestones**:  
  - **Pre-19th century**: Wood and plant oils were used for heating and lighting.  
  - **1897**: Rudolf Diesel experimented with vegetable oils in his diesel engine.  
- **Technological Advancements**:  
  - **20th century**: Biofuels like ethanol and biodiesel were developed.  
  - **21st century**: **Fourth-generation biofuels** (e.g., algae-based) aim to address land-use and supply limitations.  
- **Current Applications**:  
  - **Renewable diesel** (a "drop-in" fuel) is gaining traction, with companies like a French power equipment manufacturer switching to it for reduced emissions.  
  - Biofuels account for **3% of global transportation fuel** (2023 data).  

---

#### **6. ESG Goals and Corporate Adoption**  
- **Trends**:  
  - Companies are increasingly adopting renewable energy to meet **ESG goals**, reduce carbon footprints, and comply with regulations.  
  - **Examples**:  
    - **GPT Group**: Uses solar PV and geothermal systems.  
    - **Celestica**: Transitioned to geothermal power in Romania.  
- **Technology for Sustainability**:  
  - ESG reporting software (e.g., IBM Envizi™) helps companies track and manage renewable energy data.  

---

#### **7. Future Outlook**  
- **Technological Innovations**:  
  - Advances in **pumped storage hydropower**, **geothermal drilling**, and **biofuel feedstock** (e.g., algae) are expected to expand renewable energy access.  
- **Policy and Investment**:  
  - Global investments in renewable energy are projected to exceed **$1.5 trillion annually by 2030**, driven by climate goals and cost reductions.

This summary encapsulates the historical evolution, technological milestones, and current applications of major renewable energy sources, alongside their role in corporate sustainability and future energy transitions.
