# Renewable Energy: A Historical Overview

Renewable energy is critical to combating climate change and global warming. The use of clean energy and renewable energy resources—such as solar, wind, and hydropower—originates in early human history; how the world has harnessed power from these resources to meet its energy needs has evolved over time.

# Solar Energy History

- Efforts to harness solar power date back to ancient times, with Greeks and Romans using burning mirrors (concave mirrors) to light torches.
- The world’s first known solar collector, a device that collects solar radiation, was invented in 1767 and later used to cook food.
- The late 1800s saw the advent of the first commercial solar water heater and the first solar cell, which converts light into electricity.
- The 1950s marked the modern age of solar research with the formation of the International Solar Energy Society.
- The energy crises and spiking oil prices of the 1970s propelled solar energy development as countries invested in solar technology as an alternative to fossil fuels.
- Since the 1990s, innovation in energy production and government energy policies, such as tax incentives, have spurred the growth of the solar energy industry.
- Construction began on large-scale solar power plants, including solar thermal plants, and there was an expansion of distributed solar electricity—electricity generation at residential and business locations through rooftop-mounted solar panels.
- Solar power became a source of energy for electric vehicles, with drivers charging their cars through home rooftop systems and at solar-powered public charging stations.
- In 2022, solar photovoltaic generation reached nearly 1300 terawatt hours of electricity, up 26% from the year before.
- Companies like GPT Group have installed on-site solar photovoltaic arrays to meet ESG goals.
- Software tools are used to consolidate enterprise ESG data for analysis and reporting on energy efficiency and emissions reductions.

# Wind Energy History

- Humans have long used wind energy for mechanical purposes.
- Simple windmills in China were used to pump water, and vertical axis windmills were used to grind grain in the Middle East by 200 BC.
- Windmill-powered food production emerged in the Middle East by the 11th century.
- In Europe, windmills were adapted for industrial uses, including paper making and draining lakes.
- In the 19th century, settlers in the Americas used windmills to pump water for agriculture.
- The world’s first wind turbine was built in Scotland in 1887 by James Blyth, followed by turbines from Charles Brush and Poul la Cour in Ohio and Denmark.
- Wind energy shifted from mechanical applications to electricity generation.
- The energy crises of the 1970s heightened interest in wind energy.
- Denmark was a pioneer in wind energy development.
- In 2023, a power generation equipment manufacturer in France switched from petroleum diesel to renewable diesel for generator testing, resulting in a significant decrease in greenhouse gas emissions.
- In 2023, wind energy generation reached approximately 1000 terawatt hours.

# Hydropower History

- Hydropower has been used for centuries, initially for mechanical power (e.g., mills).
- The development of hydroelectric power plants began in the late 19th and early 20th centuries.
- Hydropower is not only used for generating electricity but also for storing energy.
- Pumped storage hydropower, dating back to the 1890s, uses reservoirs at different elevations to store energy.
- Water is pumped into an upper reservoir when power is plentiful and released to generate electricity during peak demand.

# Geothermal Energy History

- Humans have harnessed geothermal energy since at least the Paleolithic period, using hot springs for bathing.
- The first known commercial use of geothermal energy occurred in 1830 in Hot Springs, Arkansas.
- The first district heating system was installed in Boise, Idaho, in 1900, pumping water from hot springs to homes and businesses.
- The first geothermal power plant was established in Italy in 1904, powered by steam from the Larderello geothermal field.
- Historically, geothermal power plant development has been limited by natural conditions (heat, fluid, pathways).
- Technological advances may enable geothermal power plants in more locations.
- A multinational company reduced its dependence on natural gas by transitioning to geothermal power at its facility in Romania.
- Celestica also invested in on-site solar photovoltaic systems and uses software to streamline data on its renewable energy efforts.

# Bioenergy History

- People have used bioenergy throughout history by burning wood for heat and light.
- Plant oil was the primary fuel for lighting lamps before the 19th century.
- Biofuels are liquid fuels derived from biomass.
- Ethanol and biodiesel are the most common biofuels.
- Rudolf Diesel experimented with vegetable oils in his diesel engine in 1897.
- Interest in ethanol spiked following the 1970s energy crises.
- Scientists are working on using food industry waste, plant waste, and algae to overcome supply and land-use limitations.
- Renewable diesel is a "drop-in" fuel that can be used in diesel engines without blending.
