# Solar Energy: Facts and Global Trends

## Introduction

Solar energy is a renewable energy source that generates electricity without producing greenhouse gas emissions or toxic pollution. While the concept dates back to 700 BC, commercialization occurred approximately 40 years ago. The article highlights numerous facts and trends related to solar energy, its impact, and future prospects.

## Solar Energy Basics

- Solar energy refers to the light and heat radiation from the sun, harnessed to generate electricity.
- An hour and a half’s worth of solar energy reaching Earth’s surface contains enough power to meet global energy consumption for an entire year.
- The Earth has the ability and technology to depend on solar energy entirely and end the global use and consumption of fossil fuels.

## Growth and Adoption

- In 2010, solar energy represented only 0.06% of the global energy mix, rising to 1.11% within nine years.
- Solar makes up the largest proportion of growth in the renewable energy mix, growing from 0.8% in 2010 to 10.3% in 2019.
- Global solar power rose by 22% in 2020, with renewable output more than doubling since 2015.
- In 2021, Australia installed over 3,000MW of rooftop solar panels, with almost one-third of Australian households having solar panels.

## Environmental Impact

- Minimal greenhouse gas emissions are generated in a solar life cycle, with estimates ranging from 0.07 to 0.18 pounds of carbon dioxide equivalent per kilowatt-hour.
- Solar PV accounts for 3% of global electricity generation, with a record 156 TWh generated in 2020, marking a 23% growth from 2019.
- China was responsible for 75% of the increase in annual solar PV installations from 2019 to 2020.
- For a solar power plant to provide electricity for 1,000 homes, 32 acres of land are required, equivalent to 0.8% of the entire US.
- Solar thermal power plants require water use, and hazardous materials need to be disposed of correctly.

## Economic Trends and Cost Reduction

- Industry experts predict that US solar installations will double to four million by 2023, with global uptake projected to soar.
- Solar power is now the cheapest electricity in history, cheaper than coal and gas in most major countries.
- Solar costs are expected to decline by 15% to 35% by 2024, spurring further growth.

## Global Leaders and Initiatives

- The Noor Complex in Morocco is the world’s largest concentrated solar power (CSP) plant, with a 580-megawatt capacity.
- China is expected to reach 1,200GW of wind and solar capacity by 2026, four years earlier than its target.
- India aims to increase energy from renewable sources up to 50% by the end of 2030, with a goal to install 100GW of grid-connected solar power plants by 2022.
- India also aims to decentralize solar power to meet cooking, lighting, and other energy needs.

## Challenges and Disruptions

- Supply chain issues, including rising costs for steel and aluminum, global bottlenecks, and elevated shipping costs, are hindering solar energy growth.
- Pandemic lockdowns and supply chain issues have disrupted project constructions in India, resulting in less than 4GW of solar PV capacity installed in 2020.
- China has been increasing its coal use despite its pledge to carbon peak by 2030 and to reach carbon neutrality by 2060.

## Technological Advancements

- The global PV sector has been growing at an average of over 40% in the last eight years.
- Most panels today are between 15% and 20% efficient, with their resistance to extreme weather drastically improved.
- Panel durability averages about three decades with little to no loss in efficiency.

## Future Prospects: Space-Based Solar Power

- Scientists are exploring the possibility of building solar power stations in space.
- Space-based solar panels would be continuously and directly exposed to more powerful sunlight.
- Space-based solar panels could generate up to 2,000 gigawatts of power constantly, nearly 40 times more energy than a solar panel would generate on Earth annually.
- Space-based solar power is considered more efficient, clean, and available than ground-based solar stations.
