### **4. Historical Development of Climate Science**
- **Early Foundations**: 
  - **Joseph Fourier** (1820s) proposed the greenhouse effect.
  - **Eunice Newton Foote** (1856) demonstrated CO₂'s warming effect.
  - **John Tyndall** (1859) identified greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane) as heat absorbers.
- **Climate Models**: 
  - **Svante Arrhenius** (1896) projected a 5–6°C temperature rise from doubled CO₂.
  - **Gilbert Plass** (1950s) developed early computer models linking CO₂ to warming.
  - **Charles Keeling** (1958) began the "Keeling Curve," tracking rising CO₂ levels.
- **Modern Consensus**: 
  - **IPCC Reports** (since 1988) confirm human causation of climate change.
  - **2021 IPCC Assessment**: Declares human-induced climate change is "unequivocal."

### **5. Scientific Consensus and Attribution Science**
- **Consensus**: Over **99% agreement** in recent scientific literature (2019) on human-driven climate change, with no major scientific body disagreeing.
- **Extreme Event Attribution (EEA)**: 
  - Uses climate models to quantify how climate change affects specific weather events (e.g., "this heatwave was made 2°C hotter by global warming").
  - Enables "rapid attribution studies" within news cycles post-events.

### **7. Challenges and Misinformation**

  - Echo chambers in media and blogs spread misinformation.

### **8. Key Scientific Milestones**
- **1979 Charney Report**: Confirmed climate models' accuracy in predicting warming.
- **James Hansen's 1988 Testimony**: Publicly linked CO₂ emissions to global warming.
- **Keeling Curve**: Continuous data showing rising atmospheric CO₂ levels since 1958.

---

### **9. Global Impact and Projections**
- **Feedback Loops**: Warming from CO₂ increases water vapor, amplifying temperature rise (Arrhenius' theory).
- **Ocean Absorption Limits**: Oceans cannot absorb excess CO₂ indefinitely, leading to acidification and reduced heat retention.
