## **1. Climate Change and Its Impacts**

### **1.1 Environmental and Social Impacts**
- **Climate Change Effects**: Rising global temperatures, extreme weather events, sea-level rise, and biodiversity loss.
- **Human Activities**: The primary cause of climate change, with greenhouse gas emissions from fossil fuels, deforestation, and industrial processes.
- **Regional and Political Differences**: Public concern and understanding of climate change vary by region, gender, age, and political affiliation.
- **Climate Justice**: Vulnerable populations, especially in developing countries, are disproportionately affected by climate change.

### **1.2 Climate Protests and Litigation**
- **Climate Protests**: Movements like the **School Strike for Climate** (led by Greta Thunberg) and **Extinction Rebellion** demand urgent action.
- **Legal Action**: Lawsuits against governments and corporations for failing to address climate change. The **International Court of Justice (ICJ)** ruled in 2025 that states have a duty to prevent climate change and can be sued if they fail.

### **1.3 Public Awareness and Opinion**

## **2. Climate Science and Historical Context**

### **2.1 Early Scientific Insights**
- **19th Century**: Scientists like **Alexander von Humboldt**, **Joseph Fourier**, **Eunice Newton Foote**, and **John Tyndall** laid the foundation for understanding the greenhouse effect.
- **Arrhenius' Model**: In 1896, **Svante Arrhenius** published the first climate model, predicting that doubling CO₂ levels would raise global temperatures by 5–6°C.

### **2.2 Development of Climate Science**
- **1950s–1970s**: **Gilbert Plass**, **Hans Suess**, and **Roger Revelle** contributed to early climate models and the understanding of CO₂'s role.
- **1979 Charney Report**: Confirmed the accuracy of climate models and the link between human activity and global warming.
- **1988**: **James Hansen** testified before the U.S. Senate, publicly linking global warming to human activities.

### **2.3 Scientific Consensus**
- **Near Unanimity**: Over 99% of climate scientists agree that climate change is real, human-caused, and requires urgent action.
- **IPCC Role**: The **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)** has been instrumental in synthesizing scientific research and providing policy guidance.

## **3. Climate Change Attribution and Extreme Events**

### **3.1 Extreme Event Attribution (EEA)**
- **Definition**: A method to determine how much human-caused climate change has influenced specific extreme weather events.
- **Examples**:
  - "This heatwave was made m degrees hotter than it would have been in a world without global warming."
  - "This event was effectively impossible without climate change."
- **Technological Advances**: Improved computing power and climate models have enabled rapid attribution studies, often published within days of extreme weather events.
