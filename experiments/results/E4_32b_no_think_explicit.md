## **1. Climate Change and Human Activities**

- **Greenhouse Effect and Warming**:  
  - The greenhouse effect, first proposed by Joseph Fourier in the 1820s, explains how the Earth's atmosphere traps heat.  
  - Eunice Newton Foote (1856) and John Tyndall (1859) demonstrated that gases like CO₂ and methane absorb and re-radiate heat, contributing to warming.  
  - Svante Arrhenius (1896) developed the first climate model, predicting significant warming from increased CO₂ levels.  

- **Scientific Consensus**:  
  - By the 1950s–1970s, scientists like Gilbert Plass, Hans Suess, and Roger Revelle confirmed rising CO₂ levels and their warming effect.  
  - The **Keeling Curve** (1958–present) documents the steady increase in atmospheric CO₂.  
  - The **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)**, established in 1988, has confirmed with near-unanimous consensus that human activities are the primary cause of current climate change.  
  - As of 2021, the IPCC stated it is **"unequivocal"** that climate change is caused by humans.  

- **Extreme Event Attribution (EEA)**:  
  - EEA studies use climate models to determine how much human-caused climate change influences the frequency, intensity, and impact of extreme weather events.  
  - These studies enable statements like, "This heatwave was made X degrees hotter by climate change."

## **2. Public Awareness and Opinion**

- **Rise in Public Awareness**:  
  - Climate change became a public issue in the late 1980s.  
  - Media coverage in the 1990s often conflated climate change with ozone depletion.  
  - Films like *The Day After Tomorrow* (2004) and documentaries like *An Inconvenient Truth* (2006) brought attention to the issue.  

- **Demographic and Political Differences**:  
  - More educated individuals, women, and younger people tend to view climate change as a serious threat.  
  - Political partisanship and national CO₂ emissions levels influence public concern.  
  - In 2021, a majority of citizens in 30 countries expressed high worry about climate change.  
  - A 2024 global survey found that **89%** of people demand stronger political action on climate change.  

- **Climate Protests and Activism**:  
  - Movements like the **School Strike for Climate** (led by Greta Thunberg) and **Extinction Rebellion** have mobilized public demonstrations.  
  - Litigation is increasingly used to hold governments and corporations accountable for climate inaction.  
  - On **July 23, 2025**, the **International Court of Justice** issued an advisory opinion stating that states must act to prevent climate change and can be sued for failing to do so.

## **3. Climate Policy and International Agreements**

- **Paris Agreement (2015)**:  
  - A landmark international treaty aimed at limiting global warming to well below 2°C, preferably 1.5°C.  
  - Countries commit to reducing emissions and enhancing climate resilience.  

- **Litigation and Legal Action**:  
  - Lawsuits are being filed against governments and fossil fuel companies to enforce climate commitments or seek compensation for climate-related damages.  
  - The 2025 ICJ advisory opinion strengthens the legal obligation of states to act on climate change.

## **4. Climate Science and Historical Context**

- **Early Climate Science**:  
  - Scientists in the 19th century, such as Alexander von Humboldt, began to recognize the potential for human-induced climate change.  
  - The greenhouse effect was first described by Fourier, and its implications for CO₂ and water vapor were explored by Foote and Tyndall.  

- **Development of Climate Models**:  
  - In the 20th century, scientists like Gilbert Plass, Charles Keeling, and James Hansen developed models and data showing the link between CO₂ and global warming.  
  - The **Charney Report (1979)** and **Hansen's 1988 Senate testimony** were pivotal in confirming human-caused climate change.

## **5. Impacts and Responses**

- **Environmental and Social Impacts**:  
  - Climate change is causing more frequent and intense extreme weather events, rising sea levels, and biodiversity loss.  
  - Vulnerable populations and low-income countries are disproportionately affected.  

- **Mitigation and Adaptation Strategies**:  
  - Mitigation includes reducing greenhouse gas emissions through renewable energy, energy efficiency, and carbon pricing.  
  - Adaptation involves preparing for and responding to climate impacts, such as building resilient infrastructure and managing water resources.  

- **Economic and Policy Instruments**:  
  - Carbon taxes, cap-and-trade systems, and green finance are tools used to incentivize emissions reductions.  
  - International cooperation is essential for achieving global climate goals.

## **6. Future Outlook and Challenges**

- **Projected Climate Scenarios**:

  - This would lead to catastrophic impacts, including food and water shortages, mass displacement, and ecosystem collapse.  

- **Technological and Policy Innovations**:  
  - Advances in renewable energy, carbon capture, and geoengineering may offer solutions.  
  - International agreements and national policies must be strengthened to meet climate targets.  

- **Need for Global Cooperation**:  
  - Climate change is a global problem that requires coordinated action across all sectors and nations.  
  - Equity and justice must be central to climate policy to ensure that vulnerable communities are not left behind.
