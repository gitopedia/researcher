## **1. Climate Change and Its Impacts**

### **1.1 Climate Change Overview**
- **Definition**: Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperature, precipitation, and other weather patterns.
- **Human Influence**: The majority of current climate change is attributed to human activities, particularly the emission of greenhouse gases (GHGs) like CO₂ and methane.
- **Global Warming**: The Earth's average surface temperature has risen significantly since the late 19th century, with the most rapid warming occurring since the 1970s.
- **Consequences**: Includes rising sea levels, extreme weather events, biodiversity loss, and threats to food and water security.

### **1.2 Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation**
- **Mitigation**: Reducing GHG emissions through renewable energy, energy efficiency, and sustainable practices.
- **Adaptation**: Adjusting to the impacts of climate change, such as building resilient infrastructure and protecting vulnerable communities.
- **International Agreements**: The **Paris Agreement (2015)** aims to limit global warming to well below 2°C, preferably 1.5°C, compared to pre-industrial levels.

### **1.3 Climate Justice and Equity**
- **Vulnerable Populations**: Low-income and developing countries are disproportionately affected by climate change despite contributing the least to GHG emissions.
- **Climate Justice**: Calls for equitable solutions that address historical emissions and support adaptation in vulnerable regions.

## **2. Climate Change and Society**

### **2.1 Public Awareness and Opinion**
- **Awareness Growth**: Climate change became a global public issue in the late 1980s and early 2000s, driven by media and scientific reports.
- **Public Concern**: Concern about climate change has increased over time, with a majority of citizens in 30 countries expressing high worry in 2021.
- **Demographics**: Education, age, and political affiliation influence public perception and concern about climate change.

### **2.2 Climate Protests and Activism**
- **Youth Movements**: The **School Strike for Climate** (initiated by Greta Thunberg) has mobilized millions of young people globally.
- **Civil Disobedience**: Groups like **Extinction Rebellion** have used protests and civil disobedience to demand urgent climate action.
- **Legal Action**: Lawsuits against governments and fossil fuel companies have increased, with the **International Court of Justice** (ICJ) issuing an advisory opinion in 2025 that states nations must act to prevent climate change.

## **3. Climate Change and the Environment**

### **3.1 Ecological Impacts**
- **Biodiversity Loss**: Climate change threatens ecosystems and species, with some regions experiencing mass extinctions.
- **Ocean Acidification**: Increased CO₂ levels are causing ocean acidification, which harms marine life and coral reefs.
- **Extreme Weather Events**: Climate change is intensifying heatwaves, hurricanes, and droughts, increasing the risk of natural disasters.

### **3.2 Environmental Justice**
- **Disproportionate Impact**: Marginalized communities, especially in the Global South, face greater environmental risks and fewer resources to adapt.
- **Climate Migration**: Rising sea levels and extreme weather may displace millions of people, leading to climate refugees.

## **4. Climate Change and the Economy**

### **4.1 Economic Impacts**
- **Costs of Inaction**: Climate change could cost trillions in economic losses due to damage from extreme weather, health impacts, and disrupted supply chains.
- **Green Economy**: Transitioning to a low-carbon economy offers opportunities for new industries, jobs, and sustainable development.

### **4.2 Energy Transition**
- **Renewable Energy**: Solar, wind, and other renewables are becoming more cost-effective and are central to global decarbonization efforts.
- **Fossil Fuel Divestment**: Many institutions and investors are moving away from fossil fuels, signaling a shift toward sustainable energy.

## **5. Climate Change and Science**

### **5.1 Scientific Consensus**
- **Unanimous Agreement**: Over 99% of climate scientists agree that climate change is real, human-caused, and requires urgent action.
- **IPCC Reports**: The **Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC)** provides authoritative assessments of climate science and policy options.

### **5.2 Climate Modeling and Attribution Science**
- **Climate Models**: These models predict future climate scenarios based on different emission pathways.
- **Extreme Event Attribution**: Scientists now attribute specific weather events (e.g., heatwaves, floods) to climate change with increasing confidence.

### **5.3 Historical Context**
- **Early Science**: In the 19th century, scientists like **Alexander von Humboldt**, **Joseph Fourier**, and **Svante Arrhenius** laid the foundation for understanding the greenhouse effect.
- **Modern Research**: The **Keeling Curve** (1958) tracks rising CO₂ levels, providing key evidence for human-driven climate change.

## **6. Climate Change and Policy**

### **6.1 International Policy**
- **Paris Agreement**: A global treaty to limit warming and support climate action.
- **NDCs (Nationally Determined Contributions)**: Countries submit pledges to reduce emissions and adapt to climate change.

### **6.2 National and Local Policies**
- **Carbon Pricing**: Taxes or cap-and-trade systems to reduce emissions.
- **Renewable Energy Targets**: Many countries have set goals to transition to 100% renewable energy by mid-century.

## **7. Climate Change and the Future**

### **7.1 Climate Projections**
- **Worst-Case Scenario**: If emissions continue to rise, global temperatures could exceed 4°C by the end of the century.
- **Best-Case Scenario**: Rapid decarbonization could limit warming to 1.5°C, avoiding the worst impacts.

### **7.2 Call to Action**
- **Urgency**: Scientists and activists emphasize the need for immediate and sustained action to prevent irreversible climate damage.
- **Global Cooperation**: Addressing climate change requires collaboration across nations, sectors, and communities.
