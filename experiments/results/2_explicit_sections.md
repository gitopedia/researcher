# Definition and Overview
- Climate change refers to long-term shifts in temperatures and weather patterns, primarily caused by human activities.
- The primary driver of current climate change is the increase in greenhouse gases (GHGs) like CO₂ and methane.
- It is distinct from weather, which refers to short-term atmospheric conditions.
- The term "climate change" is often used interchangeably with "global warming."
- The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) defines climate change as a change in the state of the climate that is persistent for decades or longer.
- Climate change includes both global warming and its effects, such as rising sea levels, extreme weather events, and changes in precipitation.
- The Earth's average surface temperature has risen by about 1.2°C since the late 19th century.
- Climate change affects ecosystems, human health, food security, and economic stability.
- It is considered one of the most pressing global challenges of the 21st century.
- The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) aims to stabilize greenhouse gas concentrations in the atmosphere.

# Historical Background/Timeline
- The concept of climate change was first proposed in the 19th century by scientists like John Tyndall.
- In 1896, Svante Arrhenius predicted that burning fossil fuels could lead to global warming.
- The 1950s saw the first measurements of rising CO₂ levels at Mauna Loa Observatory in Hawaii.
- The 1970s saw increased scientific concern about the greenhouse effect and its potential impacts.
- In 1988, the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) was established to assess climate change science.
- The 1992 Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro led to the creation of the UNFCCC.
- The Kyoto Protocol, adopted in 1997, was the first international treaty to set binding emission reduction targets.
- The 2009 Copenhagen Climate Summit aimed to create a successor to the Kyoto Protocol but failed to produce a binding agreement.
- The 2015 Paris Agreement was a landmark treaty with the goal of limiting global warming to well below 2°C.
- The 2021 Glasgow Climate Pact at COP26 emphasized the need to accelerate climate action.

# Scientific Basis/Mechanism
- The greenhouse effect is a natural process that warms the Earth's surface.
- Greenhouse gases like CO₂, methane, and nitrous oxide trap heat in the atmosphere.
- Human activities, especially the burning of fossil fuels, have increased the concentration of these gases.
- CO₂ levels have risen from about 280 ppm in the pre-industrial era to over 420 ppm today.
- Methane is a more potent greenhouse gas than CO₂, though it remains in the atmosphere for a shorter time.
- Deforestation reduces the Earth's capacity to absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere.
- The melting of polar ice and permafrost can release additional greenhouse gases, creating feedback loops.
- Ocean acidification is a result of the oceans absorbing excess CO₂ from the atmosphere.
- Rising temperatures are causing more frequent and severe weather events, such as heatwaves and hurricanes.
- Climate models are used to predict future climate scenarios based on different emission pathways.

# Key People and Contributors
- John Tyndall (1820–1893) was one of the first scientists to study the greenhouse effect.
- Svante Arrhenius (1859–1927) predicted that burning fossil fuels could lead to global warming.
- Charles David Keeling (1928–2005) began the Mauna Loa CO₂ measurements in 1958.
- James Hansen, a NASA scientist, testified before the U.S. Congress in 1988 about global warming.
- Michael Mann is known for his work on the "hockey stick" graph showing temperature trends.
- Christiana Figueres was instrumental in negotiating the Paris Agreement in 2015.
- Greta Thunberg, a Swedish environmental activist, has raised global awareness about climate change.
- Bill McKibben, an environmental writer, founded 350.org to advocate for climate action.
- Rachel Carson, though primarily known for her work on pesticides, highlighted environmental interconnections.
- Al Gore, former U.S. Vice President, has been a vocal advocate for climate action through his documentary "An Inconvenient Truth."

# Major Events and Milestones
- 1988: The IPCC was established to assess climate change science.
- 1992: The UNFCCC was adopted at the Earth Summit in Rio de Janeiro.
- 1997: The Kyoto Protocol was adopted, setting binding emission reduction targets.
- 2005: The Kyoto Protocol entered into force after enough countries ratified it.
- 2009: The Copenhagen Climate Summit aimed to create a successor to the Kyoto Protocol but failed to produce a binding agreement.
- 2015: The Paris Agreement was adopted, with the goal of limiting global warming to well below 2°C.
- 2016: The Paris Agreement entered into force after being ratified by enough countries.
- 2018: The IPCC Special Report on Global Warming of 1.5°C warned of the dangers of exceeding that threshold.
- 2019: The European Green Deal was launched to make the EU climate-neutral by 2050.
- 2021: The Glasgow Climate Pact was adopted at COP26, emphasizing the need to accelerate climate action.

# Current Status/Recent Developments
- Global CO₂ emissions have continued to rise, though the rate of increase has slowed in recent years.
- Renewable energy sources like wind and solar have seen significant growth, but fossil fuels still dominate global energy production.
- The world is currently on track for a temperature rise of around 2.4–2.6°C by the end of the century.
- The 2020s have been the hottest decade on record, with 2023 being the hottest year globally.
- The Arctic is warming at more than twice the global average rate, leading to rapid ice loss.
- Wildfires, hurricanes, and heatwaves have become more frequent and severe in many regions.
- The concept of a "carbon budget" has gained prominence, as the remaining budget for CO₂ emissions is rapidly depleting.
- Carbon pricing mechanisms, such as carbon taxes and cap-and-trade systems, are being implemented in various countries.
- The concept of climate justice has gained traction, emphasizing the disproportionate impact of climate change on vulnerable populations.
- The role of the private sector in climate action has increased, with many companies committing to net-zero emissions.

# Impacts and Effects
- Rising sea levels threaten coastal cities and low-lying island nations.
- More frequent and intense heatwaves are increasing mortality and health risks.
- Changes in precipitation patterns are causing droughts in some regions and floods in others.
- Climate change is affecting food security through impacts on crop yields and water availability.
- Biodiversity loss is accelerating due to habitat destruction and changing climate conditions.
- Ocean acidification is harming marine ecosystems, particularly coral reefs.
- Climate change is exacerbating existing social and economic inequalities.
- Migration and displacement are increasing as people are forced to leave areas affected by climate change.
- The insurance industry is facing increased risks from climate-related disasters.
- Climate change is affecting global security through resource scarcity and conflict over water and land.

# Controversies and Debates
- There is ongoing debate about the extent of human influence on climate change.
- Some political and corporate entities have disputed the scientific consensus on climate change.
- The role of natural climate variability versus human-induced change is a topic of debate.
- There is controversy over the effectiveness and fairness of carbon pricing mechanisms.
- The debate over nuclear energy as a low-carbon solution continues.
- The use of carbon capture and storage (CCS) technology is controversial due to its high costs and uncertain effectiveness.
- The ethics of geoengineering as a potential solution to climate change are widely debated.
- There is disagreement over the pace and scale of the transition to renewable energy.
- The role of developed versus developing countries in reducing emissions is a contentious issue.
- The economic costs and benefits of climate action are often debated in political and economic circles.

# Future Outlook
- The world must achieve net-zero emissions by mid-century to limit warming to 1.5°C.
- The transition to renewable energy is expected to accelerate, with solar and wind becoming dominant energy sources.
- Advances in energy storage and grid technology will be critical for managing renewable energy.
- The role of hydrogen as a clean energy carrier is expected to grow.
- Carbon removal technologies, such as direct air capture, may become more viable in the coming decades.
- Climate adaptation strategies will become increasingly important as some level of warming is now inevitable.
- International cooperation will be essential to meet global climate goals.
- The private sector will play a key role in financing and implementing climate solutions.
- Public awareness and political will will be critical in driving climate action.
- The next few decades will determine whether the world can avoid the worst impacts of climate change.

# Related Topics
- Global Warming: The gradual increase in Earth's average surface temperature.
- Greenhouse Gases: Gases that trap heat in the atmosphere, contributing to the greenhouse effect.
- Carbon Footprint: The total amount of greenhouse gases emitted by an individual, organization, or product.
- Renewable Energy: Energy derived from naturally replenished resources, such as wind, solar, and hydro.
- Fossil Fuels: Non-renewable energy sources like coal, oil, and natural gas.
- Climate Justice: The ethical and political debate over who is responsible for climate change and who bears its consequences.
- Sustainable Development: Development that meets the needs of the present without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
- Environmental Policy: Government policies aimed at protecting the environment and addressing climate change.
- Biodiversity Loss: The decline in the variety of life on Earth, often accelerated by climate change.
- Ocean Acidification: The decrease in the pH of the Earth's oceans, caused by the absorption of CO₂ from the atmosphere.
